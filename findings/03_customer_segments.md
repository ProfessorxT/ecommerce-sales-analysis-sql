**To:** Chief Marketing Officer

**From:** Head of Growth Marketing

**Subject:** Customer Concentration & Q4 Budget Reallocation

### 1. The Concentration Reality

Our customer base is highly concentrated, creating both a growth opportunity and a retention risk. Of 10,000 registered customers, **26.14% are VIP buyers (5+ orders)**, yet they generate **78.19% of total customer revenue**. Their average customer spend is **₹75,689**, more than 12x the **₹6,288** average spend of one-time buyers.

At the other end of the funnel, **49.99% of customers have purchased only once**, but contribute just **12.42% of revenue**. This indicates that acquisition is successfully creating customers, but a significant portion of those customers is not progressing into higher-value relationships.

The takeaway for Q4 is straightforward: **growth should not be measured only by acquiring more customers; it should be measured by moving more customers from first purchase → second purchase → VIP.**

The most valuable segment is clearly the **VIP customer base**. However, the largest addressable opportunity is the **4,999 one-time buyers**. Even a modest second-order conversion from this pool could create meaningful incremental revenue without requiring equivalent acquisition spend.

The **1,187 repeat buyers** are the next strategic bridge: they have demonstrated purchase intent and should be systematically nurtured toward VIP status.

### 3. Q4 Budget Reallocation

For Q4, I recommend shifting a portion of incremental budget away from **high-CAC top-of-funnel paid search** and toward retention and lifecycle programs.

**Priority 1 — Second-order repurchase workflows:** Build automated email journeys triggered after the first purchase, using product/category affinity, replenishment timing, complementary products, and targeted incentives to drive the second order.

**Priority 2 — VIP loyalty incentives:** Protect the ₹19.79 Cr VIP revenue base with exclusive rewards, early access, personalized offers, and tier-based benefits designed to increase frequency and reduce churn.

**Priority 3 — Measurement:** Evaluate Q4 spend using **incremental second-order conversion, customer lifetime value, repeat purchase rate, and incremental revenue per marketing rupee**, rather than acquisition volume alone.

**Recommendation:** Maintain efficient acquisition, but rebalance marginal Q4 dollars toward **retention, second-order conversion, and VIP expansion** where the existing customer economics demonstrate significantly higher value.
