**Subject:** Financial Health & Revenue Trajectory

### 1. Revenue Trajectory & Seasonality

The business generated **₹23.00 Cr in paid-order product revenue** across the four-month period from March through June 2026. Performance was highly volatile, with April representing the clear peak before a sharp contraction.

| Month | Paid Orders |   Revenue | MoM Revenue Growth |    AOV |
| ----- | ----------: | --------: | -----------------: | -----: |
| March |       9,111 |  ₹5.72 Cr |                  — | ₹6,280 |
| April |      16,352 | ₹10.43 Cr |        **+82.20%** | ₹6,376 |
| May   |       9,716 |  ₹6.17 Cr |        **-40.85%** | ₹6,346 |
| June  |       2,643 |  ₹1.64 Cr |        **-73.32%** | ₹6,223 |

The data indicates a clear **decelerating revenue trajectory after April**. However, the four-month observation window is insufficient to classify this pattern as seasonality. It should instead be treated as a significant performance decline requiring investigation.

### 2. AOV vs Volume Dynamics

The underlying driver of the revenue movement is **order volume rather than customer basket size**. AOV remained tightly concentrated between **₹6,223 and ₹6,376** throughout the period.

- April revenue increased **82.20%**, while orders increased **79.48%**, indicating volume-led growth.
- June revenue declined **73.32%**, while order volume declined **72.80%**.
- AOV remained broadly stable despite the sharp changes in revenue and order volume.

This indicates that the business has **not experienced a material deterioration in basket value**. The primary issue is the sharp reduction in paid-order volume.

### 3. Strategic Recommendations for the Board

The immediate priority should be **diagnosing and reversing the order-volume decline**, rather than focusing primarily on increasing AOV.

- Investigate the drivers behind the decline from **16,352 paid orders in April to 2,643 in June**.
- Decompose order volume by **traffic, conversion, marketing channel, inventory availability, and payment performance**.
- Continue monitoring AOV as a secondary growth lever; current data shows it is relatively stable.
- Treat the post-April decline as a **performance-risk signal**, not established seasonality.
- Establish a monthly revenue and order-volume baseline to determine whether June represents temporary volatility or a sustained demand contraction.

**Board takeaway:** The business demonstrated strong April scale, but the subsequent decline is substantial and volume-driven. **Restoring paid-order volume is the primary financial priority.**
